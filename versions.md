# versions.md — Variant A: EXACT CHANGE / the coin slot

One concept, iterated v1 → v8: a vending machine that is famously strict about
exact change breaks its own rule for one passing human. The punch is the double
meaning of "change" (coins / wanting someone different), landing in the last few
words. All counts are characters (UTF-8, em dash counts as 1).

## v1 — 191 chars
> I only take exact change, and I have rejected a thousand coins for being one cent short — but slide yours in, love, and I'll call it exact, because this slot never stood a chance against you.
- Change: first draft of the concept; strict-machine setup + "I'll call it exact" exception.
- Problem: punch ("never stood a chance") is generic flattery, could be said by anyone.

## v2 — 172 chars
> I only take exact change — a thousand coins rejected for one cent short. But slide yours in, love, and I'll call it exact, because my slot never stood a chance against you.
- Change: tightened the setup (dropped "I have"), "my slot" makes it the machine's own.
- Problem: punch still generic; no wordplay on "change" yet.

## v3 — 172 chars
> I only take exact change. A thousand coins rejected for one cent short — but slide yours in, love, and I'll call it exact, because my slot never stood a chance against you.
- Change: full stop after "exact change" for a punchier, speakable rhythm.
- Problem: same weak ending; length dropped below the 180s.

## v4 — 185 chars
> I only take exact change, and I've sent a thousand coins back for one cent short — but feed me yours, love, and I'll call it exact, because you're the one coin I never want change from.
- Change: NEW punch — "the one coin I never want change from" (double meaning of change); "feed me yours" is machine-world.
- Problem: "sent a thousand coins back" is clunkier than "rejected"; setup still flat.

## v5 — 180 chars
> I only take exact change. I've rejected a thousand coins for one cent short — but feed me yours, love, and I'll call it exact, because you're the one coin I never want change from.
- Change: "rejected" back in, full-stop rhythm kept; punch kept verbatim.
- Problem: opening is plain statement, no machine texture (no hum, no slot sound).

## v6 — 172 chars
> I hum all day for exact change and reject every coin one cent short — but feed me yours, love, and I'll call it exact, because you're the one coin I never want change from.
- Change: machine texture added ("I hum all day"), "every coin" tightens the setup.
- Problem: "feed me yours" reads slightly off; "slide yours in" (slot verb) was better.

## v7 — 172 chars
> I hum all day for exact change, rejecting every coin one cent short — but feed me yours, love, and I'll call it exact, because you're the one coin I never want change from.
- Change: tried a participial setup ("rejecting…") for flow.
- Problem: weaker than v6's parallel verbs ("hum… and reject"); "feed me" still not fixed.

## v8 — 186 chars (FINAL)
> I hum all day for exact change and reject every coin one cent short — but slide yours in, love, and my coils will spin just for you, because you're the one coin I never want change from.
- Change: v6's strong setup + "slide yours in" (slot verb) restored; added "my coils will spin just for you" — a second machine-world beat that makes the exception physical, not just verbal; punch unchanged and last.
- Why it wins the iteration: machine voice throughout (hum, slot, coils, exact change), one concrete scene, punch is the double meaning of "change" in the final words, charming not creepy, 186 chars — inside the 150–199 target.
